## Block 6: Written Reflection (60 min)

Answer in a few sentences each — this is also the raw material for the presentation's closing remarks:

1. Walk through the project from Monday's schema exploration to today. What changed the most about your understanding of the data between Day 1 and now?
  Day 1 was mostly schema reconnaissance: seeing nine separate tables (orders, customers, order_items, reviews, etc.) and figuring out how they connect via order_id and customer_id. The shift by Day 4 was moving from "here are the tables" to "here's one denormalized model (main_sales_orders_detail) built to answer three specific business questions" — going from schema-first thinking to contract-first thinking, where the question drives which joins and columns actually matter, rather than trying to model everything.
2. Compare this build to Week 3's dbt project. What would change about this dashboard if you had a full week (and a dbt-style pipeline) instead of four days against a dataset you'd never seen?
   With a full week and a dbt-style pipeline, the biggest change probably wouldn't be the dashboard's visuals — it'd be the underlying discipline: staged models with tests (not-null, referential integrity on order_id/customer_id), documented sources, and a lineage graph instead of a sql/journal/ folder of day-by-day scripts. Four days against an unfamiliar dataset forces you to hand-build the equivalent of dbt's staging layer ad hoc; a week with dbt would replace that improvisation with something reproducible and testable from the start.
3. Which of today's polish decisions made the biggest visible difference for the least effort?
   Grouping the long tails into "Other" buckets (categories beyond the top 10, states beyond the top 10). It's a small transformation step but it's what makes the pie charts and the whole dashboard readable instead of a wall of tiny slices. Similarly cheap and high-impact: the delivery_bucket grouping that turned a scatter of raw delivery days into six clean bars the review-score trend line could sit on top of.
4. What's the one thing you'd do differently if this project started over today, knowing what you know now about Olist?
   Knowing the data now, I'd design the delivery-time and category groupings before writing the joins, not after. A lot of the Day 2 "update" script exists because the bucket logic got bolted on once the base model was already built. Starting with the data contract (which we only formalized in Block 2) as the very first step, rather than exploring the schema first, would have saved a rework pass.
